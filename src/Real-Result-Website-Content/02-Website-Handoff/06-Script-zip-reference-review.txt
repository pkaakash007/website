REFERENCE REVIEW & ORIGINAL COPY APPROACH

Source: the user-supplied Script.zip, containing 37 files.
Review method: inventory of all files; text extraction of the text-based documents; targeted reading of relevant passages; selected OCR pages from two image-heavy PDFs. This was not an exhaustive cover-to-cover review. Page numbers below refer to the PDF page sequence, not necessarily the printed page number.

MAIN APPLICATIONS
1. Customer first: The Complete Hooks Playbook, The Little Black Book of Copywriting Secrets, and Brand Storytelling led the opening toward the visitor’s recognizable problems and desired change.
2. A clear umbrella message: the supplied Building a StoryBrand file, especially pp. 129–138, discusses clear website offers, multiple service divisions, short text, and repeated direct actions. Marketing and development share a message, then separate into useful service paths.
3. Curiosity with a useful answer: Triggers, Hook Point, the Content Marketing Playbook, and hook guides informed specific questions about missed enquiries and repeat work. The body copy pays off the question; it does not withhold basic information to force contact.
4. Benefits and mechanism: The 16-Word Sales Letter and the copywriting technique collections informed the connection between the desired outcome and the work involved.
5. Confidence to enquire: objections about scope, cost, timing, ownership, and existing systems are answered briefly. The contact form asks for a problem, not a fully prepared technical brief.

BOUNDARIES
All site copy is original. Reference examples, financial claims, invented scarcity, unsupported psychological statistics, and promises of guaranteed growth were not carried into the copy. Historical social platform advice was not treated as current platform guidance. No real client proof, agency history, award, location, or certification was supplied.
